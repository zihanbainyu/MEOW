# What big question could the MST-back paradigm own? A theoretical review and new hypotheses

This is a theory-first document. It asks what your design is *uniquely* positioned to discover, reviews
the relevant theoretical landscape, and proposes new hypotheses generated from the paradigm itself
rather than from your current three. The goal is a flagship contribution, not an incremental one.

## The gap worth owning
Everyone agrees episodic and working memory interact. Nobody has specified **what episodic memory
actually delivers to working memory, and in what form.** The field's default is *reinstatement*: a
stored item is reactivated and read back into the current state (the whole encoding–retrieval-similarity
tradition). But reinstatement-as-replay is almost certainly the wrong model for how memory *helps you
act now*. Your paradigm has a feature no reinstatement study has: it manipulates the **computation
applied to the memory at encoding** (directed comparison of two confusable items) and then forces that
memory to be *used* under working-memory pressure. That turns your study from "does the brain replay?"
into "what representation does memory construct, and hand forward, so the present can be resolved?" —
a far bigger question.

## Theoretical landscape (where the field is, and where the room is)
- **Complementary learning systems** (McClelland, McNaughton & O'Reilly 1995; Norman & O'Reilly 2003)
  partitions a fast hippocampus and a slow cortex by *timescale*. It says nothing about memory operating
  on the sub-minute scale of working memory — the regime your task lives in.
- **The episodic buffer** (Baddeley 2000) is the field's placeholder for exactly this interface: a store
  that binds long-term content into the current workspace. Twenty-five years on it remains a box with no
  mechanism. Your design can fill it.
- **Reinstatement / encoding–retrieval similarity** (Ritchey et al. 2013; Wing et al. 2015) established
  that patterns recur — but treats memory content as a *copy* of the encoded item.
- **Differentiation vs. integration** (Favila et al. 2016; Chanales et al. 2017; Schlichting et al. 2015;
  Molitor et al. 2021; Ritvo, Turk-Browne & Norman 2019) showed learning actively *reshapes* hippocampal
  geometry — pushing similar memories apart or together, nonmonotonically with similarity. This is the
  key missing ingredient: memory is *constructed*, not stored verbatim.
- **Predictive map / successor representation** (Stachenfeld, Botvinick & Gershman 2017; Behrens et al.
  2018; Whittington et al. 2020) recast the hippocampus as predictive and generative.
- **Memory-guided attention / "premembering"** (Nobre & Stokes 2019; Stokes 2015) argues memory's purpose
  is prospective: to furnish templates "adapted to serve future behavior," not veridical records.
- **Activity-silent WM = episodic memory?** (Beukers et al. 2021) put the EM/WM boundary itself in play.

The room is obvious: no one has unified *memory-as-construction* (differentiation), *memory-as-prediction*
(successor/premembering), and *memory-in-service-of-the-present* (episodic buffer, WM) in a single
empirical paradigm. Yours can.

## Flagship thesis
**Episodic memory does not replay items into working memory; it constructs and hands forward a
comparison-derived, predictive, discriminative code — it teaches working memory the distinction it will
need, and predicts when it will be needed.** Memory's contribution to the present is an *abstraction with
a forecast*, not a copy. Everything below is a facet of this thesis, ranked by how much of a landmark
each could be on its own.

## Generated hypotheses (new questions, not reworkings of yours)

### H1 — The currency of episodic support is a *difference code*, not an item
*Claim.* What memory delivers to working memory is not the reinstated object but the **diagnostic feature
that distinguishes the pair** — a low-dimensional "difference vector" distilled by comparison. The
reinstated representation should be *more abstract and more diagnostic* than the original percept, not a
faithful copy of it.
*Stake.* Overturns reinstatement-as-replay. Connects memory's output to the attentional-template idea
that memory serves behavior, not fidelity (Nobre & Stokes 2019), and to abstract, low-dimensional neural
geometries that support generalization (Bernardi/Fusi lineage). If true, "episodic memory stores and
delivers comparisons" is a new principle.
*Why it's big.* It changes what reinstatement *is*.

### H2 — Comparison actively sculpts hippocampal geometry, and WM inherits that geometry
*Claim.* Placing two confusable items in direct comparison (your *compared* condition) **differentiates**
their hippocampal representations — moves them apart in representational space — and the magnitude of this
episodically-induced differentiation sets the later working-memory discrimination benefit. *Isolated*
encoding differentiates weakly; *novel* not at all.
*Stake.* Recasts "pattern separation" from an automatic dentate filter into a **demand- and
attention-driven, experience-dependent reshaping** of memory space (Favila et al. 2016; Chanales et al.
2017's nonmonotonic law predicts the strongest differentiation at *moderate* A–B similarity — a testable
curve your stimulus set can trace). The organism separates what it is led to distinguish.
*Why it's big.* It makes separation a controllable cognitive act, and ties the quality of a memory to the
*computation performed on it*, not its strength.

### H3 — Episodic memory supplies working memory's *prospective template*
*Claim.* Memory supports WM by **predicting the upcoming comparison and pre-instantiating the diagnostic
representation before the probe arrives**, converting discrimination from a reactive search into a
predictive match. The forward-activated content is the expected pairmate's diagnostic features, not a
generic cue.
*Stake.* Unifies episodic prediction (successor representation; Stachenfeld et al. 2017) with
memory-guided attention / premembering (Nobre & Stokes 2019): the *source* of the prospective template is
a specific episodic memory. Reframes working-memory discrimination as **active inference seeded by
episodic memory**.
*Why it's big.* It gives "memory serves the future" a concrete generative mechanism in a WM task.

### H4 — Under overload, the hippocampus becomes a working-memory-timescale episodic buffer
*Claim.* When demand exceeds working-memory capacity, the hippocampus is recruited to **maintain and bind
the overflow on the sub-minute timescale** — the very substrate the field labels "long-term" here operates
as working memory. The EM/WM distinction is a continuum of timescales, not two systems; hippocampal
engagement scales with the gap between load and capacity, and its content is the maintained diagnostic code.
*Stake.* Gives Baddeley's (2000) episodic buffer a mechanism and directly adjudicates "is activity-silent
WM simply episodic memory?" (Beukers et al. 2021), while puncturing the timescale partition of CLS.
*Why it's big.* It redraws a textbook boundary between two of cognition's core systems.

### H5 — Comparison bootstraps a transferable discriminative manifold
*Claim.* Repeated comparison-based encoding does not just sharpen individual pairs; it **builds an
abstracted discriminative dimension that generalizes to new, never-compared similar items** — memory
teaches working memory *how to tell similar things apart in general*.
*Stake.* Episodic experience as a **teacher of representational format** — bridging episodic memory,
schema/concept formation, and the emergence of low-dimensional abstract geometry that supports
generalization (cognitive-map / abstraction lineage; Behrens et al. 2018).
*Why it's big.* It makes a single-session episodic manipulation a model of how experience shapes the
format of cognition, not just its content.

### H6 — The three "stages" are one continuous memory trajectory
*Claim.* Separation (encoding), prediction (pre-probe), and completion (decision) are not three mechanisms
but **phases of a single evolving neural state** that carries a differentiated code from formation through
anticipation to use — trackable as one trajectory rather than three snapshots.
*Stake.* A dynamical reframing (Stokes 2015): memory support for the present as a continuous process, not
a sequence of lookups.
*Why it's big.* It replaces a box-and-arrow account with a trajectory — the kind of reframe that defines a
subfield's next decade.

## The paper I would build
Anchor the flagship on **H1 + H2 + H3**, because together they state one clean, novel thesis with three
converging pillars:

> Working memory does not borrow stored items from episodic memory. Episodic memory *constructs* the
> distinction the moment demands (H2), *abstracts* it into a diagnostic code (H1), and *predicts* when it
> will be needed, delivering it in advance (H3). Memory's gift to the present is a learned, forecasted
> distinction.

That is a single-sentence contribution a general journal cares about: it reconceives the EM→WM relationship
from retrieval to **construction-and-prediction**, and it reconceives pattern separation from an automatic
filter into a **goal-directed act**. H4 (the fast episodic buffer) is the natural high-risk, high-reward
second paper that redraws the EM/WM boundary; H5 (transferable discriminative manifold) is the generalization
paper; H6 is the dynamical synthesis.

The paradigm earns this because it is the rare design that manipulates *the computation applied to a memory*
(comparison vs. isolation vs. none), bridges *formation to use* within subjects, and does so *under
working-memory pressure* — exactly the conditions needed to see memory being built for, and delivered to,
the present.
